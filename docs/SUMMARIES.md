# Writing bill summaries

Summaries are what readers see first. They have to be accurate, plain and neutral.

## Rules

1. **Describe, don't judge.** Say what the bill would do. No "important," "dangerous," "long-overdue," "sweeping," or any word that tells the reader how to feel.
2. **Two to four sentences.** What it requires or prohibits, who it applies to, how it's enforced, and when it would take effect if the bill says.
3. **From the bill's text,** not from press coverage, sponsor statements or advocacy groups. If the text is unclear, say what's unclear.
4. **Plain words.** Write for a 1L. Define any term of art in a few words.
5. **No predictions** about passage or impact.
6. **Version matters.** Bills get redrafted. Summarize the version linked from the bill's page and say so if you used a redraft (for example "as reported by the Joint Committee on Advanced Information Technology").

## Example

> Requires companies that use automated systems to make hiring decisions to tell applicants and give them a way to ask for human review. Applies to employers with 50 or more employees in Massachusetts. The Attorney General would enforce it under the consumer protection law.

## Review

A second Research & Writing member reads the bill text and the summary, fixes anything inaccurate or slanted, and fills `reviewed_by` and `reviewed_on`. Summaries without review don't publish.
